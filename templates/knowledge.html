Why email for ShipMode

Email is the cheapest way to turn a one-time visitor into a signed client and keep them signed. The course argues this for consumer brands; the logic holds for a 3PL.

The course's argument: ad costs (CAC) are rising, customers are less loyal (lower LTV), and tariffs squeeze margins. Paying for 10 ad views per sale is unprofitable. Paying for one click, capturing the email, then sending 10 emails for pennies is profitable.

What changes for ShipMode:

  • Our buyer is a DTC founder or ops lead, not a shopper. They compare 3PLs for weeks before switching, so nurturing by email matters more, not less.
  • One signed client is worth months of fulfillment revenue. A small, well-qualified list beats a large one.
  • Our own clients are the market's best proof. Case studies from brands we ship for are our strongest email content.
  • Retention is our version of repeat purchase. Emails that keep clients informed (stock alerts, shipping updates, monthly reports) protect LTV.

The rule we keep from the course: we own the list. No algorithm or ad price can take it away.

The 3.5 pillars, ShipMode version

New subscribers per month matters more than total list size, because lists churn and new subscribers are the warmest. Each pillar keeps the course's principle and adjusts the execution for B2B.

PillarWhat the course saysShipMode version
1. List growthTrack new subscribers per month. A brand gaining 2,000/mo beats one sitting on 20,000.Grow with lead magnets brand operators want: a 3PL switching checklist, a fulfillment cost calculator, a peak-season prep guide. Track new qualified leads per month.
2. FlowsAutomated emails after an action. About 50% of email revenue.Automate the sales cycle: welcome, quote follow-up, onboarding, client check-ins, win-back (see Flows).
3. Campaigns2–4 sends per week of useful content so readers expect you in the inbox.Start at 1 per week (B2B buyers read less often), then test 2. Lead with education and client proof, not discounts.
3.5 DeliverabilityYour sender "credit score". Send only to engaged people and send good content.Same rule, plus required domain authentication. Treated as a setup task, not an afterthought (see Deliverability).

Tool: the course recommends Klaviyo, with Omnisend as the budget option. Its sign-up links are affiliate links. For a B2B list, compare against a B2B-focused tool before committing.

Rules for every flow:

  • Turn off Smart Sending for flows, so time-sensitive emails always go out.
  • Use conditional splits to skip emails once someone books a call or signs.
  • Plain, text-based emails often convert better and help deliverability.

Campaigns

Send one campaign a week to start, rotating four content buckets. Move to two a week once unsubscribes stay under target for a month.

3Operations insightPeak-season cutoff dates, carrier changes, inventory prep tips
4Offer or newsNew service, capacity opening, a free fulfillment audit

Writing rules:

  • Every email has one call to action.
  • Write skimmable, mobile-first copy; use a P.S. for urgency or a testimonial.
  • Sell with proof and value, not price cuts ("margin preservation").
  • A/B test subject lines first, since they decide whether anything else gets read.

Deliverability and compliance checklist

Complete these before the first send. The course calls deliverability "easy", but inbox providers now enforce several items.

  1. Send from a ShipMode subdomain, not a personal inbox.
  2. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on the sending domain.
  3. Add one-click unsubscribe and a physical address in every footer (CAN-SPAM).
  4. Collect consent at opt-in; use double opt-in for lead-magnet sign-ups.
  5. Never import bought or scraped lists.
  6. Warm up the domain: start with the most engaged contacts, then raise volume gradually.
  7. Send campaigns only to an engaged segment (opened or clicked in the last 90 days).
  8. Suppress contacts with no engagement for 90+ days (sunset).
  9. Add a preference center so contacts can choose topics instead of unsubscribing.
  10. Check spam complaints and bounces weekly against the benchmarks.

Glossary

CAC
Cost to acquire one customer (for us, one signed client)
LTV
Total revenue a customer brings over the relationship
ESP
Email service provider, such as Klaviyo or Omnisend
Flow
Automated series triggered by an action
Campaign
One-time send to a list or segment
Transactional email
Order, shipping or account email; not marketing, but can be optimized
Segment
Dynamic group that updates as contacts meet conditions
List
Static group, usually from one source
Engaged segment
Contacts who opened or clicked in the last 14/30/60/90 days
Suppression list
Contacts who must never be emailed again
Conditional split
Rule that sends contacts down different paths in a flow
Smart Sending
Klaviyo setting that skips recently emailed contacts; turn it off for flows
CTR / CTOR
Clicks per recipient / clicks per opener
RPR
Revenue per recipient
Hard / soft bounce
Permanent / temporary delivery failure
SPF, DKIM, DMARC
Email authentication records that prove the sender is real
Sender reputation
Inbox providers' trust score for your domain
Warmup
Raising send volume gradually to build or rebuild reputation
Double opt-in
Subscriber confirms through a second email
CAN-SPAM / GDPR / CASL
US / EU / Canada email and consent laws
Content buckets
Rotating themes for campaigns
Offer ladder
Moving from a low-friction opt-in to higher-value offers
Trust threshold
The point a prospect trusts you enough to buy
Margin preservation
Selling with value and proof instead of discounts
